# Instagram Engagement Analytics
## Outlier-Sensitive Business Intelligence and Engagement Analysis

## 1. Project Overview

This project delivers an end-to-end Business Intelligence solution for analyzing Instagram post performance across content categories, media types, traffic sources, exposure measures, and reporting periods.

The project transforms post-level Instagram analytics data into a structured MySQL data warehouse and interactive Power BI reporting environment. It combines data cleaning, KPI engineering, dimensional modelling, exploratory analysis, and outlier detection to support more reliable interpretation of content performance.
The workflow integrates:

A central objective is to distinguish between:

- **Interaction volume:** Total engagement generated by posts.
- **Audience exposure:** Reach and impressions.
- **Relative engagement performance:** Source-provided engagement rate.
- **Typical versus exceptional performance:** All-post results compared with non-outlier results.

The analysis tests whether apparent engagement-rate differences across content categories, media types, and traffic sources remain meaningful after excluding statistically unusual engagement-rate observations.

> **Important:** This is a descriptive analytics project. Results identify patterns and associations within the supplied dataset but do not establish causal relationships between content characteristics and performance outcomes.

---

## 2. Business Questions

This project investigates the following questions:

- What are the overall levels of engagement, reach, impressions, and follower acquisition within the portfolio?
- How does performance vary across content categories, media types, and traffic sources?
- How do category, media-type, and traffic-source rankings change when engagement rate is measured before and after outlier exclusion?
- How are reach, impressions, total engagement, and engagement rate descriptively related?
- Are unusually high engagement-rate observations concentrated at particular impression levels or within particular content segments?
- How does total engagement change over time, and which months show notable growth or decline?
- Can raw engagement-rate averages be used reliably as indicators of typical content performance?
- How can Power BI dashboards support consistent monitoring without overstating causal conclusions?

## 5. ETL Pipeline and Architecture

The project uses an Extract–Transform–Load workflow to create an analysis-ready warehouse and reporting layer.

```text
Instagram_Analytics.csv
        ↓
     Extract.py
        ↓
data/staging/instagram_raw.csv
        ↓
   transform.py
        ↓
Processed dimension and fact CSV files
        ↓
      Load.py
        ↓
   MySQL warehouse
        ↓
  Power BI dashboards
```

### 5.1 Extract — `Extract.py`

The extraction script:

- Reads the source dataset.
- Dynamically resolves project paths.
- Creates the staging directory when needed.
- Saves a raw snapshot as `instagram_raw.csv`.
- Preserves the extracted source data before any cleaning or transformation.

This staging layer supports traceability, reproducibility, and comparison between raw and processed data.

### 5.2 Transform — `transform.py`

The transformation script prepares the dataset for warehouse loading and BI analysis.

Key transformations include:

- Median imputation for missing numerical values.
- Mode imputation for missing categorical values.
- Date conversion and removal of invalid publication dates.
- Standardization of categorical labels.
- Duplicate removal using `post_id`.
- Total-engagement calculation.
- Monthly reporting-period creation.
- Month-over-month engagement-growth calculation.
- High-engagement flag creation.
- Average engagement by media type.
- IQR-based engagement-rate outlier detection.
- Construction of dimension and fact tables.
- Exploratory correlation and outlier-adjusted category analysis.
- Final fact-table null validation.

### 5.3 Load — `Load.py`

The loading script:

- Retrieves MySQL credentials through environment variables.
- Creates MySQL dimension and fact tables.
- Applies CSV-to-database column mappings.
- Loads dimension tables before the fact table.
- Recreates the fact table during refresh.
- Uses parameterized SQL inserts.
- Converts Pandas missing values to SQL `NULL`.
- Closes database connections and cursors after execution.

## 6. Data Warehouse Design

The processed data is structured into a star-schema-oriented MySQL model.

```text
              Time_Dim
                 │
Content_Dim ── Instagram_Fact ── Media_Dim
                 │
             Traffic_Dim
```

### Dimension Tables

| Table | Purpose |
|---|---|
| `Time_Dim` | Publication date, year, month, and day attributes |
| `Content_Dim` | Content category, caption length, hashtag count, and related content attributes |
| `Media_Dim` | Media-format reference values |
| `Traffic_Dim` | Traffic-source reference values |

### Fact Table

`Instagram_Fact` stores one record per post and includes:

- Likes, comments, shares, and saves.
- Total engagement.
- Reach and impressions.
- Followers gained.
- Engagement rate.
- Monthly engagement-growth rate.
- High-engagement flag.
- Average engagement by media type.
- Dimension keys.
- IQR-based outlier flag (`is_outlier`).

The `is_outlier` field is persisted in the warehouse rather than being used to delete records. This enables Power BI to compare all posts, non-outlier posts, and outlier posts within one reproducible data model.

## 7. Analytical Methodology

### 7.1 Total Engagement

Total engagement is calculated as:

\[
\text{Total Engagement} =
\text{Likes} + \text{Comments} + \text{Shares} + \text{Saves}
\]

This metric measures absolute interaction volume. It is useful for identifying high-volume posts and portfolio-level interaction scale but does not measure engagement efficiency relative to exposure.

### 7.2 IQR-Based Outlier Detection

Engagement-rate outliers are identified using the conventional 1.5 × IQR rule.

```python
Q1 = df["engagement_rate"].quantile(0.25)
Q3 = df["engagement_rate"].quantile(0.75)

IQR = Q3 - Q1

lower_bound = Q1 - 1.5 * IQR
upper_bound = Q3 + 1.5 * IQR

df["is_outlier"] = (
    ~df["engagement_rate"].between(lower_bound, upper_bound)
).astype(int)
```

The method identifies engagement-rate observations outside the calculated bounds.

**Result:** **2,557 posts**, or approximately **8.5%** of the dataset, were flagged as engagement-rate outliers.

The outlier flag indicates statistical unusualness; it does not prove that a post is invalid, erroneous, or commercially unimportant. Outliers may represent exceptional content, low exposure denominators, campaign activity, or data-quality issues. They are retained for analysis rather than removed permanently.

### 7.3 Categorical Association Screening

Content categories and traffic sources are one-hot encoded before calculating their correlation with engagement rate.

```python
dummies = pd.get_dummies(
    df[["content_category", "traffic_source"]]
)

corr = (
    dummies.corrwith(df["engagement_rate"])
    .sort_values(key=abs, ascending=False)
)
```

The strongest positive association is observed for Beauty:

\[
r = 0.0139
\]

The strongest negative association is observed for External traffic:

\[
r = -0.0077
\]

All coefficients are below:

\[
|r| < 0.014
\]

This indicates negligible standalone linear association between engagement rate and the category or traffic-source indicators examined.

Correlation screening is descriptive only. It does not account for media type, reach, impressions, post timing, account characteristics, paid promotion, creative quality, campaign context, or interaction effects.

### 7.4 Comparative Analysis

Power BI compares performance across:

- All posts.
- Non-outlier posts.
- Outlier posts.
- Content categories.
- Media types.
- Traffic sources.
- Reporting periods.
- Individual post records.

The analysis also uses log-transformed engagement rate to reduce the visual distortion caused by extreme values and to examine the distribution of engagement rate relative to impressions.

### 7.5 Core DAX Measures

```DAX
Avg Engagement (All) =
AVERAGE(instagram_fact[engagement_rate])
```

```DAX
Avg Engagement (No Outliers) =
CALCULATE(
    AVERAGE(instagram_fact[engagement_rate]),
    instagram_fact[is_outlier] = 0
)
```

```DAX
Outlier Post Count =
CALCULATE(
    COUNTROWS(instagram_fact),
    instagram_fact[is_outlier] = 1
)
```

```DAX
% Outlier Posts =
DIVIDE(
    CALCULATE(
        COUNTROWS(instagram_fact),
        instagram_fact[is_outlier] = 1
    ),
    COUNTROWS(instagram_fact)
)
```

## 9. Key Findings

- The portfolio contains **29,999 posts** and generated approximately **3B total engagements**, **30B reach**, and **37B impressions**.
- **2,557 posts (8.5%)** were flagged as engagement-rate outliers using the IQR method.
- Excluding outliers reduced average engagement rate from **14.42% to 9.95%** (approximately **31% reduction**), indicating that extreme observations materially inflate the raw average.
- Category rankings changed substantially after outlier exclusion: Beauty moved from 1st to approximately 6th, while Food moved to 1st. The category range narrowed from **1.91 to 0.62 percentage points**.
- Media-type and traffic-source differences also narrowed after outlier removal, with ranges decreasing from **0.43 to 0.15** and **0.89 to 0.32 percentage points**, respectively.
- One-hot correlation analysis found negligible linear associations between engagement rate and content-category or traffic-source indicators (\(|r| < 0.014\)).
- Outliers were concentrated at lower impression levels and unusually high engagement rates, while non-outliers occupied a higher-impression, more moderate-rate region.
- Month-over-month engagement growth was volatile, with large movements in December 2024 (+157.6%) and November 2025 (−36.4%), and limited sustained momentum during most of 2025.

## 10. Business Recommendations

- Use the non-outlier average engagement rate (**9.95%**) as the primary benchmark for typical performance, while retaining the raw average (**14.42%**) as a full-population measure.
- Do not use raw category, media-type, or traffic-source rankings alone to allocate budget or define strategy; treat observed differences as testing priorities rather than confirmed winners.
- Investigate top-engagement posts and high engagement-rate outliers separately to identify potentially repeatable characteristics and distinguish exceptional from typical performance.
- Maintain diversified media and traffic-source strategies; robust averages are tightly clustered around 10%, and small raw differences should not drive major reallocations.
- Investigate major monthly changes (e.g., December 2024 increase and November 2025 decline) using post volume, content mix, campaign activity, and outlier proportion.
- Separate reporting into scale (total engagement, reach, impressions) and efficiency (non-outlier engagement rate, median, percentiles) views.
- Enhance dashboard governance by documenting metric definitions, formatting outlier proportion as a percentage, and adding median, percentile, and post-level drill-through capabilities.

## 11. Limitations

- The dataset is externally sourced and may not represent a validated Instagram Business or Creator account extract.
- The engagement-rate formula and denominator are not explicitly documented.
- IQR-based outlier detection identifies statistically unusual observations; it does not determine whether a post is erroneous or commercially unimportant.
- Alternative outlier methods or thresholds may produce different classifications and rankings.
- Correlation analysis identifies unadjusted linear associations only and does not establish causality.
- The analysis does not control for paid promotion, campaign objective, audience demographics, account size, content quality, post timing, competitor activity, or external events.
- Aggregate reach does not represent unique users across the full portfolio.
- Impressions include repeated exposure and do not represent unique views.
- The time period is insufficient to establish reliable recurring seasonality.
- Mean-based metrics remain sensitive to distribution shape; median and percentile measures should complement averages.
- The project does not include conversion, revenue, campaign cost, sales, leads, website traffic, or return-on-investment measures.

## 13. Conclusion

This project demonstrates how a Python–MySQL–Power BI workflow can support reproducible Instagram performance analysis across engagement, exposure, time, category, media format, and traffic source.

The central finding is that engagement-rate outliers materially influence raw averages and rankings. Excluding 2,557 flagged posts reduces average engagement rate from **14.42% to 9.95%**, while category, media-type, and traffic-source differences become substantially smaller.

The recommended approach is to report both raw and outlier-adjusted metrics, monitor scale and efficiency separately, investigate exceptional posts as a distinct population, and use observed differences to prioritize controlled testing rather than infer causal content strategy.
